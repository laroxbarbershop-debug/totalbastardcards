# GAME DESIGN DOCUMENT — VISUAL DIRECTION

## Core Vision

This game is a premium digital recreation of a physical Top Trumps-style card game.

The player should feel like they are looking down onto a real card table from above.

The cards are the focus.

The experience should feel:
- tactile
- premium
- strategic
- suspenseful
- clean
- polished

This is not a dashboard.
This is not a typical mobile game interface.
This is not a menu-driven card battler.

The player should feel like they are handling real cards on a table.

---

# Camera Perspective

## Top-Down Bird's Eye View

The entire game is viewed from directly above.

The screen represents a shared tabletop.

There are:
- no player avatars
- no characters
- no faces
- no first-person perspective
- no opponent representation except their cards, score and card collection

The focus is the tabletop battle.

The cards are the characters.

---

# Overall Aesthetic

The visual style should resemble:

- premium collectible card games
- luxury card rooms
- high-quality tabletop games
- physical playing cards brought to life digitally

The feeling should be closer to:
- a beautiful board game
- a luxury card set
- a collector's item

Avoid:
- cartoon game interfaces
- bright mobile game aesthetics
- cluttered UI
- generic web application styling
- excessive fantasy elements

---

# Colour Direction

Primary environment:

- deep charcoal
- dark wood
- black felt
- subtle textures

Accent colours:

- metallic gold
- warm highlights
- subtle reds for important actions

The background should support the cards.

The cards should be the brightest and most detailed objects on screen.

---

# Game Table

The playing area should feel like a physical surface.

Possible background:

- dark felt
- premium gaming table
- subtle fabric texture
- soft lighting

The table should have depth.

Cards should cast subtle shadows.

Objects should feel like they exist physically on the surface.

---

# Layout

The screen is divided into two player areas.

## Opponent Area

Position:

Top of screen.

Contains:

- Player 2 score
- Player 2 captured card stack
- Opponent card position

---

## Battle Area

Centre of screen.

Contains:

- Opponent card (face down)
- Player card (face up)
- Reveal animation area

The two active cards are the main focus.

---

## Player Area

Position:

Bottom of screen.

Contains:

- Player 1 score
- Player 1 captured card stack
- Player's active card
- Draw deck

---

# Active Cards

During a round:

## Player Card

- Face up.
- Fully visible.
- Large enough to read all attributes.
- Slightly closer to the player.

The player should always know their available choices.

---

## Opponent Card

- Face down before selection.
- Premium card back design.
- Positioned opposite the player's card.
- Hidden information creates suspense.

After attribute selection:

The card flips smoothly to reveal the opponent's stats.

The reveal should be a major moment.

---

# Card Flip Animation

The card reveal should feel like turning over a physical card.

Sequence:

1. Player selects an attribute.
2. Selected attribute highlights.
3. Brief anticipation pause.
4. Opponent card flips.
5. Both cards display the chosen attribute.
6. Winner is highlighted.
7. Cards move to the winner's collection.

Animation should feel satisfying, not excessive.

Avoid:
- explosions
- cartoon effects
- unnecessary particles

The drama comes from the reveal.

---

# Draw Deck

Position:

Bottom centre.

The deck should appear as a physical stack of cards.

Requirements:

- Face down.
- Slight elevation.
- Visible shadow.
- Remaining card count displayed.
- Subtle interaction animation when drawing.

When a card is drawn:

- Card lifts from the deck.
- Slides into position.
- Deck count decreases.

---

# Captured Card Stacks

Winning cards are placed into the player's collection pile.

## Player 1

Position:

Bottom left corner.

## Player 2

Position:

Top right corner.

The stacks should look physical.

Cards should:

- remain face up
- slightly overlap
- be offset slightly
- show enough of each card to recognise the growing collection

Do not create a flat list.

They should look like real piles of collected cards.

---

# Card Design

Cards are the most important visual element.

Each card should feel collectible.

Card contains:

- artwork
- card name
- five attributes:
  - Notoriety
  - Money
  - Strength
  - Intelligence
  - Fame

Design priorities:

1. Artwork
2. Card identity
3. Attribute readability
4. Premium finish

---

# Attribute Display

Attributes should be:

- easy to scan
- visually balanced
- elegant

Avoid spreadsheet-like tables.

Use:
- icons
- clean typography
- clear numbers

The chosen attribute should visually stand out.

Example:

Chosen:

NOTORIETY: 87

should become highlighted.

---

# Winning / Losing Feedback

Winner:

- subtle glow
- highlighted number
- satisfying movement

Loser:

- reduced emphasis
- secondary animation

Do not overdo effects.

The game should feel confident and premium.

---

# Typography

Typography should feel modern and expensive.

Use:

- bold headings
- clean numbers
- readable labels

Avoid:

- playful fonts
- comic styles
- excessive decoration

Numbers are important and should be instantly readable.

---

# Interface Rules

The interface should stay minimal.

Only show information the player needs:

- current cards
- score
- deck count
- available attributes
- captured cards

Avoid unnecessary menus during gameplay.

The table is the interface.

---

# Animation Philosophy

Animations should communicate physical movement.

Good:

- sliding cards
- flipping cards
- stacking cards
- subtle shadows
- smooth transitions

Bad:

- bouncing buttons
- excessive screen effects
- arcade-style rewards

Every animation should make the player feel like they are interacting with real cards.

---

# Design Consistency Rule

Whenever adding new features:

Maintain the established visual language.

Do not introduce:
- new colours
- new button styles
- new card styles
- new layouts

without matching the existing design system.

The priority is consistency and premium presentation.

The game should feel like a finished physical card product converted into a digital experience.
